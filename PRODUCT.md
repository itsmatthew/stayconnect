# Product Brief: Now & Then

## Problem This Solves and For Whom
The majority of people are poor at staying connected with close friends and friend groups as life pulls them apart with changes. 
New things take up space in their brain, and without reminders or specific reasons, they don't reach out. People fail to find a
 middle ground as they view staying connected as indirect interaction (like/comment on a facebook feed) or long, intense catch ups.
 Mainstream social media has lost its original purpose with addictive algorithms and inauthentic photo culture. Now & Then solves
 this for young adults going through inevitable transitions in life.

## Specific User
Maya Chen, 22, is a senior at a mid-size state school, about to start an associate marketing role in Chicago — a city where she
 knows nobody. She's spent four years building a tight couple of friend groups of 5-6 people each through her sorority and a
 study-abroad semester in Barcelona. She has 40,000+ photos on her phone and hasn't looked at 90% of them since the day she took
 them. In three weeks she moves. Her friend group already has a "we'll always be close!" energy in their group chat, but she's
 seen this before — her older sister's friend group from college went quiet within a year of graduating, and Maya's friend group
 from high school barely speaks anymore. Maya doesn't want that to happen to these groups, but also doesn't know what to actually
 do differently.

## Core Experience

### Day One Experience
 Maya has already gotten the full sorority friend group to sign up on Now & Then and half of the study abroad one. She swipes and
 opens her daily 5 photos (feature that resurfaces five random photos from camera roll every day). The first photo is a screenshot
 of a text sent to her volleyball coach from six years ago, the second photo is a picture from a social at a bar freshman year, the 
third photo is a picture from a family ski trip eight years ago, the fourth photo is a piece of paper with scribbles that she 
doesn't know what is from, and the fifth photo is a photo of her and her friend on a tractor in middle school. She sees the text 
below prompt her to share the photos, and she decides to send the one from freshman year to her sorority group. She notices that
 the message disappears from her own view until a recipient replies, removing the "did they see it and ignore it" anxiety that my
 research identified as a blocker. She also wants to share the one from the ski trip with her family, but her family is not on
 the app yet. So she immediately sends an invite to her family group chat on iMessage.

### Why They Come Back  
Maya has now been posting her weekly photo for four weeks now. She loves to see what her friends are up to, and she especially
loves the fun new experiences it has subconsciously encouraged people in her circle to have. Last week, one of her friends posted
 a picture of her skydiving! She also loves the personal ornaments/stickers that her closest groups can choose to represent 
their tree/circle. For her sorority group, all three symbols represent inside jokes that have remained in conversation for
over three years now. Also, these symbols personal to each group remain at the top of the group chat while texting. One time 
last week, her sorority friend group was fighting about the latest episode of a new show and it got more heated than it had to.
In the middle of the fight, Maya decided to send one of the stickers which was an inside joke from freshman year that always
 made them laugh. It lightened the mood and they stopped fighting. The next morning, she opened the app and saw a present 
animation pop up and there was a gift under the tree/circle of her sorority friend group. She opened it and it was a picture of
them a year ago today, getting ready for a football game at their school. There was a note attached that said,"remember how
funny Harper was acting this day?". This made Maya happy. 

## What We're Deliberately Not Doing
Now & Then will not have like or follower count. We are prioritizing users mental health while promoting authentic connection 
rather than a popularity contest. Now & Then will have full privacy assured with no ads. This is our core thesis. We are not
creating an app that requires many minutes a day to receive value. 

##Open Questions
Generally speaking, only a fraction of young adults' photos live on their phone. The majority of photos from growing up sit on
a family pc or elsewhere. Is there any way to implement these family pc photos to the daily 5 old photos? How would this work
through the mobile phone onboarding? Also, is there a way to have people join as a group straight from other social media/group
chats on other apps? A link to join as a group and already be a group immediately in Now & Then?
